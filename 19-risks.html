Chapter 08 · Candor

The model is honest by omission — here's what it leaves out, on purpose.

Every projection in this deck is a unit-economics and infrastructure model, not a full P&L. The financial workbook says this explicitly, on its own front page — we're repeating it here so it isn't missed.

01

Customer acquisition cost

The ramp from 10 to 500 users (p.14) is a growth assumption, not a marketing-spend model. What it costs to actually win each of those users is not in these numbers — see the Ask on p.18.

02

Churn and refunds

Every user in the model is assumed to stay subscribed. Real subscription businesses lose customers monthly; the net-new curve here doesn't net anything out.

03

Free-trial burn

If a free trial is offered, non-converting trial users would consume research and cover-credit costs without producing revenue — not reflected here.

04

Support cost & founder time

Customer support, account issues, and the founder's own hours are real costs of running the business that aren't billed against these margins.

We're showing this page deliberately. A model that quietly assumes away every hard problem isn't more convincing — it's less trustworthy. Every number on the preceding pages is a real, defensible unit economic; this is the honest list of what still needs to be layered on top before it's a company-level P&L.
